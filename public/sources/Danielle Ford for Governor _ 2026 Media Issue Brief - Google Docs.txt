​Danielle Ford for Governor | 2026 Media Issue Brief​
​Candidate:​​Danielle Ford​
​Office:​​Governor of Nevada​
​Election:​​2026 General Election​
​Party Affiliation:​​Nonpartisan / No Political Party​
​Current Elected Office:​​Nevada State Board of Education​
​Former Elected Office:​​Clark County School District​​Trustee​
​Campaign Website:​​DanielleForNevada.com​
​Danielle Ford is not affiliated with the Democratic or Republican Party.​

​Candidate Overview​
​Danielle Ford is a Nevada-first nonpartisan candidate for Governor of Nevada in 2026.​
​She currently serves as an elected member of the Nevada State Board of Education and​
​previously served as a Clark County School District Trustee. Danielle is running without a​
​political party affiliation because she believes Nevada’s challenges are too connected to be​
​solved through party loyalty, donor networks, or national talking points.​
​Her campaign is focused on practical systems repair: making government more transparent,​
i​mproving the systems families rely on, protecting constitutional rights, following public money,​
​and making decisions based on Nevada’s needs.​

​Core Priorities​
​1. People Over Politics​
​Danielle believes government should serve people before parties, donors, vendors, or​
i​nstitutions protecting themselves.​
​Her approach is to judge ideas by whether they solve the problem, not which political side they​
​came from. She supports more transparency in campaign finance, more meaningful choices for​
​voters, and public access to information about all legally qualified candidates.​
​Danielle also believes Nevada’s growing number of nonpartisan voters should not be treated as​
​an afterthought in a system built around two party primaries.​
​Core position:​​Public decisions should be based on​​Nevada’s needs, not party instructions.​

​2. Education​
​Danielle’s public service began in education, but her education platform is not limited to schools​
​alone. She believes schools are often expected to absorb failures from other systems: housing,​
​hunger, healthcare, mental health, transportation, family support, and public safety.​
​Danielle supports stronger funding for students with disabilities, English learners, educators,​
​paraprofessionals, school nurses, counselors, social workers, bus drivers, cafeteria workers,​
​custodians, and other school support staff.​
​She supports public education, real family options, and public accountability for every public​
​dollar.​
​Core position:​​Nevada should fund the students we​​have, not the system we wish we had.​

​3. Affordability & Opportunity​
​Danielle believes Nevada cannot call growth successful if the people who live and work here​
​cannot afford to stay.​
​Affordability is connected to housing, wages, utilities, insurance, food, childcare, transportation,​
​water, energy, infrastructure, small business, and economic development. Danielle supports​
i​ncreasing attainable housing, protecting pathways to homeownership, scrutinizing corporate​
​housing concentration, supporting small businesses, preparing workers for AI-driven changes,​
​and requiring public benefit from major economic-development deals.​
​Core position:​​Growth should make Nevada more livable,​​not less.​

​4. Health & Care Systems​
​Danielle believes families should not have to become unpaid case managers during the hardest​
​moments of their lives.​
​Her health and care platform includes healthcare access, Medicaid, mental health care,​
​addiction treatment, disability services, long-term care, rehabilitation, caregiving, safe staffing,​
​nutrition, family stability, and crisis prevention.​
​She believes Nevada must stop treating care work as invisible labor when the entire system​
​depends on it.​

​Core position:​​Care should begin before crisis becomes the only door left open.​

​5. Safer Communities​
​Danielle supports accountability for serious harm, protection for victims, and strong public safety​
​systems.​
​She also believes police cannot be expected to solve every failure that begins somewhere else.​
​Mental health, addiction, homelessness, family instability, school failure, and lack of treatment​
​often show up later as public safety problems. By then, officers are left responding to crises that​
​should have been addressed earlier.​
​Danielle supports law enforcement while also strengthening mental health care, addiction​
​treatment, crisis response, youth intervention, victim services, corrections oversight, and​
​prevention.​
​Core position:​​Safer communities require both accountability​​and functioning systems around​
​public safety.​

​6. Government That Works​
​Danielle believes government should not ask the public for trust it has not earned.​
​Her government-accountability platform focuses on public records, open meetings,​
​procurement, competitive bidding, no-bid contracts, vendor influence, whistleblower protection,​
​campaign finance, public communication, AI procurement, and measurable outcomes.​
​Danielle believes public money belongs to the public. Nevada should close loopholes that avoid​
​real competition, scrutinize major contracts, disclose who benefits, and tie public spending to​
​measurable results.​
​Core position:​​Accountability is not obstruction.​​It is part of governing well.​

​Specific Issue Positions​
​Abortion and Reproductive Rights​

​Danielle supports reproductive freedom and believes deeply personal medical decisions should​
​be made by patients and their doctors, not politicians.​
​She supports Nevada’s current legal framework protecting abortion access through 24 weeks,​
​with later care available when medically necessary to preserve the life or health of the pregnant​
​patient. She also supports protecting access to contraception, miscarriage care, fertility care,​
​and emergency medical treatment.​
​Nevada law generally permits abortion through 24 weeks, and after 24 weeks when a physician​
​has reasonable cause to believe the abortion is necessary to preserve the life or health of the​
​pregnant person.​
​Danielle’s position:​​Personal medical decisions should​​stay between patients and doctors, not​
​be controlled by politicians.​

​Voter ID and Proof of Citizenship​
​Danielle believes only eligible U.S. citizens should vote. She supports secure elections,​
​accurate voter rolls, identity verification, penalties for fraud, and transparent audits.​
​But she does not support turning voter registration into a paperwork trap for eligible citizens.​
​Many lawful voters can face barriers because of name changes, missing documents,​
​transportation, cost, age, disability, tribal access issues, or corrected records.​
​Any ID or document requirement must be constitutional, funded, accessible, carefully phased in,​
​and designed so eligible voters are not blocked from participating.​
​Danielle’s position:​​Nevada should protect election​​integrity and voter access at the same​
​time.​

​Gun Safety and Constitutional Rights​
​Danielle supports lawful gun ownership and respects responsible gun owners who train, store,​
​and carry safely.​
​She also believes gun rights come with public responsibility. Nevada should enforce background​
​checks, strengthen crisis intervention, support training and safe storage, and keep weapons that​
​are already heavily restricted under state and federal law out of the wrong hands.​
​Nevada law includes firearm-related provisions including background-check requirements for​
​certain transfers, concealed-carry permitting, and high-risk protection orders. Machine guns are​
​separately restricted under state and federal law.​

​Danielle’s position:​​Safety and constitutional rights should not be treated as opposites.​

​Taxes, Revenue, and the State Budget​
​Danielle does not believe new taxes should be the first answer to every budget problem.​
​Before asking Nevadans to pay more, the state should take a harder look at public contracts,​
​procurement loopholes, no-bid arrangements, tax abatements, vendor influence, administrative​
​waste, and whether public dollars are producing public value.​
​Danielle is also open to studying a state lottery if it is transparent, voter-approved where​
​required, and structured so the public can clearly see who benefits and where the money goes.​
​Nevada’s Constitution currently restricts lotteries, so any serious proposal would need to​
​address the legal process honestly.​
​Danielle’s position:​​Public money belongs to the public,​​and budgets should serve people, not​
i​nsiders.​

​NV Energy and Utility Rates​
​Danielle believes Nevadans deserve clearer explanations, stronger ratepayer protection, and​
​more scrutiny of long-term energy decisions.​
​The Governor does not directly set electric rates. Those decisions go through Nevada’s​
​utility-regulation process. But a Governor can appoint leaders, demand transparency, support​
​consumer protection, question long-term infrastructure costs, and push for policies that keep​
​energy reliable and affordable.​
​NV Energy is a regulated utility, and utility matters are overseen through Nevada’s Public​
​Utilities Commission process. Nevada has also debated energy-choice proposals in prior​
​elections.​
​Danielle’s position:​​Nevadans should not need to be​​energy experts to understand why their​
​bills are going up or who benefits from major utility decisions.​

​Water and the Colorado River​
​Danielle believes Nevada’s water future requires honesty, innovation, conservation, and​
l​ong-term planning.​

​Water decisions cannot be separated from growth, housing, agriculture, tribal and rural​
​communities, data centers, energy development, and the Colorado River. Nevada cannot solve​
​the Colorado River alone, but it can be more disciplined about what kind of growth it encourages​
​and what public benefits are promised in exchange for public resources.​
​Danielle’s position:​​Nevada should protect its water​​future by tying growth and​
​economic-development decisions to transparent water planning and realistic long-term costs.​

​Data Centers and AI Infrastructure​
​Danielle supports innovation, but she does not support giving away public benefits before​
​Nevada fully understands the public costs.​
​Before expanding data center tax abatements or infrastructure commitments, Nevada should​
​require clear public information about water use, energy demand, grid impacts, backup power,​
​emissions, infrastructure costs, permanent Nevada jobs, ratepayer impacts, and enforceable​
​public benefits.​
​Nevada law allows data center companies to apply for partial abatements from certain taxes,​
​and GOED describes data center abatements as available for qualifying companies that locate​
​or expand in Nevada.​
​Danielle’s position:​​If technology companies want​​public support, they should be willing to​
​provide public answers.​

​ICE, Immigration, and State Data Privacy​
​Danielle believes Nevada must follow the law, protect due process, and keep public safety​
​focused on serious harm.​
​Immigration enforcement is primarily federal, but Nevada still has responsibility for how state​
​agencies, schools, data systems, and local partnerships operate within the law. Danielle does​
​not believe undocumented status alone should be treated the same as violent crime or​
​organized criminal activity.​
​She supports clear state policies on data sharing, warrants, subpoenas, due process, school​
​safety, and public accountability. Schools should remain focused on educating children, and​
​families should not be afraid to send their children to school.​
​Danielle’s position:​​Nevada can respect the law, protect​​constitutional rights, and focus​
​enforcement priorities on serious harm.​

​Read by Grade 3 and Literacy​
​Danielle believes every child should be reading well by third grade. She does not believe​
​mandatory retention should be treated as the main solution when the system failed to intervene​
​early enough.​
​Holding a child back affects more than reading. It can affect math, science, art, confidence,​
​friendships, social development, and long-term engagement in school. Danielle supports earlier​
​screening, reading specialists and interventionists, tutoring, family book access, community​
​reading programs, libraries, after-school and summer support, special education screening,​
​English learner support, and standards-aligned educational content wherever families can​
​access it.​
​Nevada’s Department of Education states that AB 289 removed the Grade 3 mandatory​
​retention requirement from Read by Grade 3.​
​Danielle’s position:​​Children should not pay the price​​for adult systems waiting too long to​
​respond.​

​Education Funding, Special Education, and English Learners​
​Danielle believes school funding should reflect the real cost of serving students well.​
​That means Nevada must take special education, English learner support, staffing,​
​transportation, paraprofessionals, school support staff, and student safety seriously. Funding​
​averages are not enough if classrooms do not have the people and services students actually​
​need.​
​Danielle supports stronger support for special education classrooms, safe staffing conditions,​
​better family communication, language access, and programs that help English learners learn​
​English while continuing to grow academically.​
​Danielle’s position:​​Nevada should fund the students​​we have, not the system we wish we​
​had.​

​Public Safety, Mental Health, and Addiction​
​Danielle supports accountability for serious harm, protection for victims, and strong public safety​
​systems.​

​She also believes police cannot be expected to solve every failure that begins somewhere else.​
​Mental health, addiction, homelessness, family instability, school failure, and lack of treatment​
​often show up later as public safety problems. By then, officers are left responding to crises that​
​should have been addressed earlier.​
​Danielle supports law enforcement while also strengthening mental health care, addiction​
​treatment, crisis response, youth intervention, victim services, corrections oversight, and​
​prevention.​
​Danielle’s position:​​Supporting police means building​​the systems around them so they are not​
​expected to solve every failure alone.​

​Housing and Affordability​
​Danielle believes Nevada cannot call growth successful if the people who live and work here​
​cannot afford to stay.​
​Housing affordability is connected to wages, rent, utilities, insurance, transportation, childcare,​
l​and use, infrastructure, water, and corporate concentration. Danielle supports increasing​
​attainable housing, protecting pathways to homeownership, scrutinizing corporate ownership​
​and build-to-rent practices, improving consumer protection, and making sure growth pays​
​attention to roads, schools, utilities, and community impact.​
​Danielle’s position:​​Nevada should not build an economy​​where working families are priced​
​out of the state they helped build.​

​Campaign Finance, Candidate Transparency, and Open Primaries​
​Danielle believes voters deserve more choices and more transparency.​
​Political money should not be the gatekeeper for who gets heard, who gets taken seriously, or​
​who appears in voter information. Danielle supports stronger campaign finance transparency,​
l​egally durable reforms that reduce the influence of money, and public access to information​
​about all legally qualified candidates.​
​She also supports reforms that give nonpartisan voters more meaningful participation. Nevada’s​
​growing number of nonpartisan voters should not be treated as an afterthought in a system built​
​around two party primaries.​
​Danielle’s position:​​Voters deserve information about​​all legally qualified candidates, not just​
​the candidates with the most money or party infrastructure.​

​AI in Schools, Surveillance, and Pricing Algorithms​
​Danielle uses and teaches AI, and she believes technology can help solve real problems. But​
​she also believes Nevada needs guardrails before harm becomes normal.​
​AI and automated systems can affect students, workers, consumers, patients, renters, drivers,​
​and families without people realizing a machine helped shape the outcome. Danielle supports​
​transparency, privacy protections, human review, bias testing, student data safeguards,​
​procurement standards, retention limits, and public accountability before government agencies​
​adopt powerful surveillance or automated decision systems.​
​Federal FERPA protections govern many student education records, and automated license​
​plate reader systems have raised national concerns around retention, sharing, misuse, false​
​alerts, and oversight.​
​Danielle’s position:​​Innovation should serve people.​​It should not create systems no one can​
​question, audit, or control.​

​Summary for Voter Guides and Candidate Comparisons​
​Danielle Ford is a nonpartisan candidate for Governor of Nevada running on a systems-repair​
​platform.​
​Danielle’s campaign does not fit neatly into one party platform. She supports reproductive​
​freedom, public education, due process, voter access, and stronger care systems. She also​
​supports lawful gun ownership, public safety accountability, secure elections, no new taxes as a​
​first resort, contract scrutiny, procurement reform, and stronger oversight of public spending.​
​Her nonpartisan position is not that “both sides are the same.” Her position is that Nevada’s​
​problems are connected, and government should make decisions based on people, evidence,​
​public accountability, and Nevada’s long-term future.​
