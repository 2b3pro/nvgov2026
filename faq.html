Before anything else: check that you are still registered

Look yourself up at VOTE.NV.gov now, not in November. Counties mark a voter inactive when an address-confirmation notice comes back unanswered or undeliverable. Inactive voters can still vote, but they do not receive a mail ballot, and a registration can be canceled after two missed federal general elections.[1]Nevada Secretary of State, Maintaining Nevada's List of Registered Voters As of August 2026 the state counted 426,577 inactive voters, nearly half of them nonpartisan.[2]Nevada Secretary of State, voter registration report, September 3, 2026

  • • Status says inactive: update your address on VOTE.NV.gov to be restored to active. If it is too late for a mail ballot, vote in person.[3]Nevada Secretary of State, Maintaining Nevada's List of Registered Voters
  • • Status says canceled or not found: submit a new registration. You can still do this in person at any polling place through Election Day with a Nevada driver's license or ID.[4]Nevada Secretary of State, Maintaining Nevada's List of Registered Voters[5]Nevada Secretary of State, Election FAQs
  • • Moved, changed your name, or your signature has changed: update the registration so the address and signature on file match what you will put on the envelope.[6]Nevada Secretary of State, voter registration report, September 3, 2026
  • • First-time voters: if your registration data did not match DMV or Social Security records and you did not answer the county's letter, you may have to include a copy of your ID with your mail ballot.[7]Nevada Secretary of State, Mail Ballot Voting

Voting Logistics

When is the election, and when will we know who won?

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026, with early voting October 17 through 30. Unofficial results post at Results.NV.gov after polls close. Counties must finish their canvass by November 13 and the Supreme Court canvasses statewide results November 24; nothing is official before then.[16]Nevada Secretary of State, 2026 Election Information[17]Nevada Secretary of State, Election FAQs Because mailed ballots can arrive for four days after the election, close races in Nevada often take days to call.

How do I register, or check that I am registered?

Use VOTE.NV.gov to register, update, or check your status. You must be a U.S. citizen, 18 by Election Day, a resident of your county for 30 days and your precinct for 10, and not currently imprisoned for a felony.[18]Nevada Secretary of State, Election FAQs Paper registrations must be postmarked by October 6. Online registration stays open through Election Day, and same-day registration is available in person during early voting and on Election Day.[19]Ballotpedia, Voting in 2026, Nevada deadlines[20]Nevada Secretary of State, Election FAQs

Will I get a mail ballot, and how do I return it?

Yes, unless you opted out. Since 2021 every active registered voter receives a mail ballot; you must be registered at least 14 days before the election to get one.[21]Nevada Secretary of State, Election FAQs (AB 321, NRS 293.269911) The return envelope is postage-paid; sign it on the signature line. Mailed ballots must be postmarked by November 3 and received by 5 p.m. on the fourth day after. Ballots taken to an official drop box, vote center, or the clerk's office must arrive by 7 p.m. on Election Day. The Secretary of State recommends mailing at least a week early and tracking your ballot at MyBallot.NV.gov.[22]Nevada Secretary of State, Election FAQs[23]Nevada Secretary of State, 2026 Election Information Do not hand your ballot to a canvasser or organization; the state advises returning it yourself.[24]Nevada Secretary of State, Election FAQs

What if my signature does not match?

Nevada does not check ID for mail ballots; it compares the signature on your return envelope to the one on file from your registration or DMV record.[25]The Nevada Independent, 2026 ballot measure tracker (current law relies on signature matching) Sign the envelope the way you normally sign, on the signature line, and do not let anyone sign for you. If the county cannot match the signature, the ballot is challenged and the county contacts you so you can confirm it is yours. That cure window is short: in the June primary it closed at 5 p.m. six days after Election Day, so expect a similar deadline around November 9 and confirm with your clerk.[26]Nevada Secretary of State, Election FAQs (primary cure deadline) Track your ballot at MyBallot.NV.gov so you learn about a problem while there is still time to fix it.[27]Nevada Secretary of State, 2026 Election Information If your signature has changed since you registered, update your registration before you vote.

Do I need ID to vote?

Not to cast a ballot. Nevada verifies voters by matching signatures.[28]The Nevada Independent, 2026 ballot measure tracker, July 31, 2026 You do need a valid Nevada driver's license or Nevada ID card (or a tribal ID meeting state requirements) to register or update your registration on the same day you vote. A passport or military ID does not work for same-day registration, and if the address on your Nevada ID is out of date you also need proof of residence such as a utility bill or lease.[29]Nevada Secretary of State, Election FAQs Question 7 on this ballot would add a photo ID requirement to the state constitution if it passes a second time.[30]The Nevada Independent, 2026 ballot measure tracker

What does "None of these candidates" do?

Nevada has put that line on every statewide race since 1975. Votes for it are counted and published alongside the candidates' totals, but only votes for named candidates decide the winner. Choosing it is a recorded protest, not a way to force a new election, and you may mark it only if you have not voted for a candidate in that race.[31]NRS 293.269

What else is on the statewide ballot?

Two constitutional amendments getting their second and final vote, since Nevada amendments must pass in two consecutive general elections. Question 6 would write the state's existing abortion law, which permits the procedure through 24 weeks, into the constitution; it passed in 2024 with 64.4 percent. Question 7 would require government-issued photo ID to vote in person and the last four digits of a driver's license or Social Security number for mail ballots; it passed in 2024 with 73.2 percent.[32]The Nevada Independent, 2026 ballot measure tracker, July 31, 2026 Proposed measures on open primaries and independent redistricting did not qualify.[33]The Nevada Independent, 2026 ballot measure tracker The Secretary of State publishes an official ballot question guide.[34]Nevada Secretary of State, 2026 Election Information See our reproductive rights page for the candidates' positions.

The Race

Who is on the ballot for governor?

Three names, in this order, followed by "None of these candidates":[35]Washoe County Registrar of Voters, 2026 General Election contest list

  • • Ford, Aaron D. (Democratic): attorney general, former Senate majority leader. Won the June primary with 63 percent.[36]The Nevada Independent, 2026 Primary Election Results
  • • Ford, Danielle (No Political Party): State Board of Education member, former CCSD trustee.[37]Ballotpedia, Danielle Ford
  • • Lombardo, Joseph (Republican): incumbent governor, former Clark County sheriff. Won the June primary with 91 percent.[38]The Nevada Independent, 2026 Primary Election Results

Are Aaron Ford and Danielle Ford related?

No. They share a surname and appear back to back on the ballot, which reporters and a Nevada Appeal columnist flagged as a possible source of confusion when she filed.[39]Nevada Current, March 16, 2026[40]Nevada Appeal, March 21, 2026 Check the party column: DEM is Aaron Ford, NPP is Danielle Ford. On this site, "Ford" without a first name means Aaron Ford.

Why wasn't Danielle Ford in the June primary?

Nevada's primaries choose party nominees, and only registered Democrats and Republicans vote in them. She filed with no party, which skips the primary; nonpartisan candidates reach the general ballot by collecting 250 valid signatures.[41]The Nevada Independent, March 12, 2026[42]KTNV, March 12, 2026 Six other no-party candidates filed and later withdrew or were disqualified.[43]Ballotpedia, Danielle Ford

What do the polls say?

Public polling has been limited and partisan-commissioned. A July 2026 survey for the Democratic Governors Association had Lombardo and Aaron Ford tied at 44 percent with 12 percent undecided; Republican-commissioned polls that summer showed Lombardo ahead by 7 to 12 points.[44]The Nevada Independent, July 22, 2026[45]The Nevada Independent, August 2026 GOP polls No public poll has tested Danielle Ford by name, so her effect on the race is unmeasured.[46]The Nevada Independent, July 22, 2026 (two-way result) Read any poll's sponsor before its topline.

Who has the money?

Lombardo, by a wide margin. Mid-July reports showed roughly $15 million across his campaign and allied PACs against about $2.6 million for Aaron Ford, whose campaign emphasized nearly 13,000 small donors.[47]Las Vegas Sun, August 16, 2026 We located no reporting of Danielle Ford's totals.[48]Las Vegas Sun, August 16, 2026 (money race coverage) Details are on each candidate profile and in the comparison hub.

This Site

Who runs this site, and who pays for it?

It is a public-service project with no advertising, no sponsors, and no donors, and no affiliation with any candidate, campaign, party, or government body. Research and drafting use AI models under human editorial review; the models and their roles are listed on the methodology page. Every factual claim carries a citation you can hover or click.

Is the site really nonpartisan?

We apply the same tests to every candidate: sourced record, stated platform, funding, and documented contradictions between the two. We do not endorse, predict, or rate candidates. Where the evidence is asymmetric we say so rather than manufacture balance, and we publish what we could not verify. The standards and their limits are spelled out under Our Methodology.

Why do the comparison tables show only Lombardo and Aaron Ford?

The tables compare statewide records: vetoes, lawsuits, legislative votes, appointments, and donors. Danielle Ford has no statewide record, and her platform states principles on most issues rather than positions that can be set beside the nominees'. She has a full profile equal in depth to the others, with her platform mapped to the site's issue pages. If she publishes specific positions, we will add them.

How do the citations work?

Bracketed numbers link to entries on the sources page; hovering shows the source name. Sources are grouped by candidate and topic with letter prefixes (L for Lombardo, F for Aaron Ford, DF for Danielle Ford, G for general election, V for voting rules). Full research documents for each candidate are linked at the top of that page.
