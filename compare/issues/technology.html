Technology, Surveillance & Data Centers

Flock Safety cameras, police surveillance, power grid strains, water cooling, and data privacy.

The Stakes

Nevada has rapidly become ground zero for high-stakes conflicts at the intersection of emerging technology, public infrastructure, and civil liberties. The 2026 governor will directly shape whether private foundations can deploy mass surveillance technology without legislative approval, whether data centers drain Nevada's power grid and water supply, and what privacy guardrails protect residents against algorithmic tracking.

Mass Surveillance & ALPRs

More than 200 Flock Safety automated license plate reader (ALPR) cameras are scanning public streets in Southern Nevada. Acquired through a $6.3M private donation to the LVMPD Foundation, the network bypassed normal municipal budget hearings, prompting calls from Latino lawmakers and civil liberties advocates for a statewide suspension.[1]Las Vegas Sun, August 2026[2]Nevada Latino Legislative Caucus

Grid & Water Constraints

NV Energy projects data centers will surge from ~5% of total retail sales today to nearly 64% by 2046, with 22 gigawatts of commercial inquiries.[3]NV Energy 2026 IRP Filing Evaporative cooling systems consume millions of gallons of water in the Mojave Desert, sparking battles over tax abatements and utility rate hikes.

Data Privacy & AI Guardrails

With Nevada enacting health privacy laws (SB 370) and securing major location-tracking settlements ($391M Google multistate suit), candidates disagree on whether to enact rigorous statutory guardrails on automated decision-making or preserve a light-touch regulatory environment.[4]Nevada AG Google Settlement[5]Nevada SB 370

The Nevada Landscape

1. Flock Cameras & Police Surveillance

  • • Scale: LVMPD operates ~200 Flock cameras across the Las Vegas Valley, logging license plates, vehicle color, make, and unique markers without a warrant.
  • • Private Funding Bypass: The system was largely funded by ~$6.3M in gifts from the Horowitz Family Foundation to the non-profit LVMPD Foundation ("Friends of Metro"), bypassing public city and county council hearings.[6]Las Vegas Sun
  • • School Perimeters: Clark County School District (CCSD) accessed the network until pausing use in August 2026 following community outcry.[7]The Nevada Independent
  • • Out-of-State Sharing Risk: Nevada lacks state laws limiting data retention or barring out-of-state agencies from accessing plate data to investigate abortion or immigration status.

2. Data Centers & Resource Impacts

  • • Explosive Growth: Tech campuses (Switch, Google, Apple, AWS) receive substantial property and sales tax abatements through GOED under 2015 legislation.[8]IndyTalks Forum
  • • Power Demand: 22 GW in prospective data center interest threatens to require billions in new transmission lines, raising questions about whether commercial tech pays its fair share or passes costs to residential ratepayers.[9]PUCN Docket 24-05043
  • • Evaporative Cooling: Traditional data centers consume vast amounts of potable water for cooling, competing with residential communities for Colorado River allocations.

3. Legislative & Regulatory Action

  • • 2027 ALPR Bill: In August 2026, the Legislature's interim Judiciary Committee voted unanimously to request legislation establishing statewide ALPR rules, audit trails, and reporting.[10]Las Vegas Review-Journal
  • • Suspension Push: The Nevada Latino Legislative Caucus called for an immediate pause on law enforcement use of Flock cameras until safeguards exist.[11]Latino Caucus Statement
  • • Consumer Protections: Nevada’s SB 370 took effect in 2024, creating one of the nation's strictest consumer health data privacy regimes.[12]SB 370 Overview

Tradeoffs Voters Must Weigh

The Lombardo Tradeoff

Lombardo's approach maximizes law enforcement capability and economic competitiveness, but relies on private foundations that bypass council scrutiny and risks overburdening Nevada's strained electrical grid and water supplies without statutory ratepayer protections.

The Aaron Ford Tradeoff

Ford's platform offers aggressive consumer, water, and civil liberties protections, but his abrupt pivot from co-sponsoring 2015 data center tax breaks to freezing them in 2026 invites accusations of political opportunism that could chill tech sector capital investment.

The Danielle Ford Tradeoff

Danielle Ford brings sharp focus to the civil liberties and ratepayer risks of AI and surveillance, but her platform articulates broad principles rather than concrete legislative mechanisms, tax rates, or statutory enforcement thresholds.
