The Stakes: Community Trust vs. Federal Deputization

⚖️ Local Police Discretion & The 10th Amendment

Under the Supremacy Clause and Arizona v. United States (2012), immigration enforcement is fundamentally a federal responsibility. However, under the 10th Amendment's anti-commandeering doctrine, federal authorities cannot compel state and local police to enforce civil immigration violations.[IMM1]Immigration Systems Analysis

Nevada state law establishes no mandatory statewide sanctuary policy and no blanket prohibition against ICE cooperation. Consequently, every sheriff and police chief exercises independent discretion over whether to sign federal agreements, share jail records, or hold non-citizens on non-judicial detainers.

🤝 The Community Trust Imperative

The core controversy over Section 287(g) agreements centers on community policing trust. When immigrant residents perceive local municipal police or county sheriffs as an arm of federal deportation authorities, the public safety of entire neighborhoods is compromised:

  • Crime victims remain silent: Victims of domestic violence, sexual assault, and violent crimes refuse to dial 911 out of fear that officer arrival will result in deportation or family separation.
  • Witness cooperation evaporates: Key witnesses to felony offenses decline to speak with investigators or testify in court, impairing case clearance rates.
  • AG Model Guidance: Nevada AG Aaron Ford's 72-page Model Immigration Policies warn: "If people in Nevada are afraid to interact with police based on fear of immigration enforcement, then our criminal justice system loses access to witnesses and victims will be deterred from reporting crimes."[IMM4]AG Model Policies

🚔 Jail Deputization (WSO) vs. Street Policing

Federal ICE policy offers different 287(g) models. Four Nevada agencies currently participate in the Warrant Service Officer (WSO) model: Las Vegas Metro (LVMPD), Douglas County, Mineral County, and Nye County.[IMM7]U.S. ICE 287(g) Roster

Proponents like Gov. Lombardo and Sheriff Kevin McMahill argue that jail-based WSO agreements preserve street-level community trust because patrol officers do not question residents about citizenship on neighborhood beats. However, civil rights advocates point out that the street-to-jail arrest pipeline—where minor traffic or misdemeanor stops land individuals in county jails where 287(g) officers process them—produced a 30-fold explosion in Metro ICE detainers (957 through August 2025 vs. 32 in 2024).[IMM1]Immigration Systems Analysis

🏛️ The Dillon's Rule Legal Showdown

In October 2025, the ACLU of Nevada filed Morais-Hechavarria v. LVMPD in Clark County District Court.[IMM3]ACLU Lawsuit The lawsuit asserts that under Nevada's Dillon's Rule—which dictates local governments possess only powers expressly delegated by the Legislature—sheriffs lack statutory authority to execute 287(g) agreements without legislative permission. If successful, the ruling could void all 287(g) agreements statewide.

Tradeoffs Voters Must Weigh

The Lombardo Tradeoff

Lombardo’s approach facilitates the rapid transfer of foreign-born criminal arrestees to federal custody, but critics argue that even jail-based 287(g) agreements funnel minor misdemeanor arrestees into deportation, creating pervasive fear that discourages immigrant victims from reporting crimes.

The Aaron Ford Tradeoff

Ford’s model policies establish strong constitutional principles protecting community trust and due process, but his insistence that he opposes "sanctuary policies" and reliance on nonbinding guidance leaves local sheriffs free to sign 287(g) agreements without state consequences.

The Danielle Ford Tradeoff

Danielle Ford brings sharp focus to the community trust risks of police entanglement and the legal necessity of Dillon's Rule authorization, but her platform articulates broad principles rather than concrete legislative mechanics for managing inter-agency conflicts.

Questions for Town Halls & Debates

  1. 1. To Gov. Joe Lombardo: When Las Vegas Metro rejoined 287(g) in June 2025, detainer requests surged 30-fold to 957 through August. Given that crime victims and domestic violence survivors often share households with undocumented family members, how do you measure whether 287(g) partnerships deter vulnerable residents from calling 911? If the state courts rule under Dillon's Rule that local sheriffs lack statutory power to sign 287(g) agreements, will you introduce legislation to grant that power?
  2. 2. To AG Aaron Ford: Your office's 72-page Model Immigration Policies recommend that local law enforcement refuse to act as unpaid immigration agents or execute civil detainers. Yet because those guidelines are nonbinding, four Nevada agencies—including Metro—signed 287(g) agreements anyway. Why haven't you issued a formal, binding Attorney General opinion determining whether 287(g) agreements violate Nevada's Dillon's Rule?
  3. 3. To Danielle Ford: You have called for ending local police entanglement in federal deportation without clear statutory authority. If elected Governor, would you sign a state law explicitly prohibiting Nevada sheriffs from entering 287(g) agreements, or would you support a county-by-county option subject to local commission votes?
  4. 4. To All Candidates: Immigrants make up nearly one in four Nevada workers and 9% of the state's workforce is undocumented. If federal authorities launch large-scale worksite enforcement operations targeting the hospitality and construction sectors, what specific role—if any—should the Nevada National Guard and Nevada State Police play?
