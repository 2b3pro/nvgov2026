Transparency & Ethics

Dark money, public records, and government accountability.

The Stakes

Nevada's political history has long been shaped by intense concentrations of private economic influence—from early railroad and mining syndicates to resort-casino operators, developer associations, and the burgeoning artificial intelligence data center sector. In a state where 120-day biennial legislative sessions create compressed windows for policy and budgeting, executive branch transparency, procurement integrity, and campaign finance oversight are decisive safeguards against favoritism.

The 2026 gubernatorial election features candidates with starkly documented conflicts: an incumbent governor who fought the state ethics commission and pioneered 501(c)(4) dark money inaugural committees; an attorney general whose office accepted over $140,000 in corporate-sponsored travel while awarding tens of millions in legal fees to former law firm partners; and an independent challenger whose entire platform was forged through public whistleblower fights against school district vendor contracts.

Nevada's Transparency & Ethics Landscape

Statutory Oversight Guardrails

  • • Nevada Public Records Act (NRS 239): Presumption of public access to government records unless specifically sealed by statutory exemption
  • • Open Meeting Law (NRS 241): Mandates advance notice, public comment, and transparent deliberations for state and local governing boards
  • • Nevada Commission on Ethics (NRS 281A): Eight-member quasi-judicial state body empowered to investigate conflicts of interest and misuse of public office[TR1]Ethics Commission
  • • State Purchasing Act (NRS 333): Formal competitive bidding requirements for state agency contracts exceeding statutory thresholds

The Active Loopholes

  • • 501(c)(4) Inaugural Shadow PACs: Inaugural committees formed as social welfare nonprofits shield corporate and billionaire donors from disclosure[TR3]Service First Fund
  • • Sole-Source & Emergency Waivers: Agencies routinely bypass competitive procurement through administrative exceptions
  • • Contingency Fee State Litigation: Outside law firms receive tens of millions in state contract fees from AG-led settlements without legislative appropriation
  • • Disappearing Messaging & Executive Privilege: Use of Signal and WhatsApp auto-delete on personal and state devices evades public records preservation

Joe Lombardo

Republican · Incumbent

Evidence: Strong

Ethics Commission Battle & Dark Money Innovation

Lombardo's ethics record is marked by two defining controversies: a multi-year legal clash with the Nevada Commission on Ethics over using his sheriff's uniform and badge in campaign ads,[TR1]Ethics Settlement and the creation of the "Service First Fund"—a 501(c)(4) dark money nonprofit organized from his inaugural committee that accepted undisclosed corporate checks to fund attack ads against political opponents.[TR3]The Nevada Independent

The Ethics & Legal Defense Fund

In 2023, the Nevada Ethics Commission found Lombardo committed two willful violations of state ethics law for using his official badge and uniform in campaign imagery, facing up to $1.6M in civil fines. Lombardo responded by suing the Ethics Commission in district court, challenging the constitutionality of its structure. In 2025, the case settled: Lombardo agreed to a $5,000 fine for a "non-willful" violation, while using a donor-backed Legal Defense Fund to pay hundreds of thousands in private legal fees.[TR1]Ethics Case

  • • Vetoed SB 60 (2023): Rejected bill requiring inaugural committees to disclose donors[TR2]SB 60 Veto
  • • Service First Fund: Leveraged 501(c)(4) dark money for political television attacks
  • • Executive privilege: Sued news outlets to block release of executive correspondence

Defense & Administration Position

Lombardo's team argued that the Ethics Commission was weaponized by partisan actors, that wearing his uniform accurately depicted his public service background, and that his legal challenge protected executive separation of powers. On government efficiency, he established the Office of Federal Assistance and signed executive orders eliminating hundreds of obsolete administrative regulations.

"Our administration has cut red tape, streamlined government bureaucracy, and protected the constitutional boundaries of the executive branch."

Key Contradiction: Businesslike Accountability vs. Dark Money Opacity

Lombardo ran promising a clean, businesslike administration free from backroom dealmaking. Yet in office, he vetoed bipartisan inaugural disclosure (SB 60), created a dark money 501(c)(4) entity to shelter corporate campaign donors, used donor-financed legal defense funds to battle state ethics regulators, and asserted broad executive secrecy over public records.

Signature Platform: "People Over Politics" & "Government That Works"

Danielle Ford places systemic ethics and procurement reform at the center of her platform, grounded in her four-year experience as a Clark County School District trustee exposing vendor contracts, no-bid consulting deals, and closed-door buyout agreements.[DF2]Platform Pillars[DF33]Media Issue Brief

  • • Dark money ban: Complete public disclosure of all inaugural and transition committee funds

Electoral Openness & Nonpartisan Participation

Ford argues government secrecy is sustained by closed partisan primaries that disenfranchise Nevada's largest voting bloc: over 700,000 nonpartisan registered voters. She demands open primaries and equal access to statewide televised debates for qualified independent candidates.

Core principle: "When government contracts and campaign funds are hidden from the public, taxpayers end up footing the bill for insider deals."

Reality Check: Governance Without a Party Caucus

Ford brings authentic whistleblower credentials from her CCSD battles over Superintendent Jara's termination and district contracts. However, as an independent governor without a legislative caucus, implementing sweeping reforms like creating an independent inspector general or opening primary elections would require passing bills through a partisan legislature that historically benefits from existing fundraising and contracting rules.

Critical Gaps in Government Oversight

The Executive Immunity Loophole

Under current Nevada law, public officials challenged by the Ethics Commission can establish private "legal defense funds" to accept unlimited contributions from donors to pay attorneys, shielding themselves from the financial consequences of ethics inquiries. Neither major party nominee has supported closing this donor-funded legal defense loophole.

Enforcing Transparency Without a Majority

Danielle Ford's comprehensive anti-corruption platform targets the precise mechanisms of state opacity (sole-source contracts, disappearing texts, closed primaries). However, Nevada's executive branch operates under strict statutory limits. Without legislative sponsors willing to curtail their own party's fundraising advantages, an independent governor would face immense institutional headwinds in Carson City.
